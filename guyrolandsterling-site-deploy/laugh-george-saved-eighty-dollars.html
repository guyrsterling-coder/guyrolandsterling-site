The Day George Saved Eighty Dollars
Pull up a chair. I've got a story for you.

Now, I don't know if you've noticed this...
...but there are certain jobs a man decides to do exactly one minute after finding out what somebody else charges to do them.
Oil changes are one of those jobs.

It never starts with,
"I'd really enjoy spending my Saturday underneath a truck."
No.
It starts with somebody saying,
"Eighty dollars."
Suddenly every man in America becomes a certified mechanic.
That's how this story begins.

My friend George walked over one afternoon wearing the smile that usually meant my day was no longer my own.
"I'm done paying people to change my oil."
I nodded.
"Who's paying them?"
"I am."
"Well, there's your first mistake."

George ignored me.
"I watched three videos."
"Only three?"
"That's all it takes."
I looked toward heaven for strength.
"You know... there are surgeons who've watched more than three videos."
He waved me off.
"It's just draining oil."

Those words...
...have started more expensive afternoons than anyone cares to admit.

Off we went to the auto parts store.
George already had a shopping basket.
That should have worried me.

Into the basket went six quarts of motor oil.
A new filter.
A drain pan.
A giant socket.
A longer ratchet.
A funnel.
Rubber gloves.
Shop towels.
A flashlight.
A creeper.
A pair of wheel chocks because, as George explained,
"You can never be too safe."

I quietly noticed he'd forgotten one small thing.
"What kind of oil does your truck take?"
He stopped.
"Hmm."
"You know... that might've been useful information."

Back inside we went.
Turns out he'd grabbed the wrong oil.
Back on the shelf it went.
New oil.
Different filter.
Another fifteen minutes gone.

By the time we got back to his house, George looked as proud as if he'd rebuilt the engine already.
He drove the truck onto the ramps.
Almost.

The first attempt missed them entirely.
The second climbed one ramp but not the other.
The third... well... let's just say the ramps survived.

Finally everything was in place.
George slid underneath.
"Hand me the wrench."
A few seconds passed.
"Hmm."
"What?"
"I think somebody tightened this."

I stared at the underside of the truck.
"You mean... the last mechanic?"
George grunted.
"This ain't right."

For the next ten minutes he pulled.
Pushed.
Kicked.
Invented three new body positions no chiropractor would recommend.
Nothing.

Finally...
CRACK!
"There!"
The drain plug broke loose.
"So did your knuckles," I observed.

A thin red line appeared across the back of his hand.
George looked at it.
"It'll wash off."
"It usually does."

Then came the moment every do-it-yourself mechanic waits for.
He spun the drain plug out with his fingers.
Only... he forgot one tiny detail.
Hot oil comes out immediately.

It didn't politely wait.
It shot straight across his hand...
...ran down his arm...
...filled his sleeve...
...splashed into the drain pan...
...and somehow completely missed half the pan altogether.

George let out a noise I've only heard from men stepping barefoot on Legos.

He tried catching the plug.
The plug escaped.
It bounced once.
Twice.
Rolled across the garage floor.
Then disappeared into the darkest corner of the building.

We both stared after it.
"You remember where it went?"
"No."
"You?"
"No."
George sighed.
"Well... it's around here somewhere."

Twenty minutes later we'd found it.
Right after finding three golf balls...
...an old screwdriver...
...two fishing lures...
...and a socket George had been missing since 2018.

Sometimes cleaning your garage just requires losing something new.

The oil finally finished draining.
George screwed the plug back in.
Installed the new filter.
Poured in the fresh oil.
Started the truck.
It purred beautifully.

George folded his arms.
"There."
"I told you."
I had to admit... everything sounded perfect.

Then I noticed something.
A slow...
steady...
black drip...
landing on the garage floor.
Drip.
Drip.
Drip.

I pointed underneath.
"George..."
He leaned down.
"Oh."
Apparently he'd forgotten to tighten the oil filter.

He shut the engine off.
Oil covered everything he'd just cleaned.
He tightened the filter.
Started it again.
Perfect.
No leaks.

George grinned.
"See? I knew I could do it."
"I never doubted you'd eventually get there."
He looked proud enough to frame the receipt.

That's when I asked the question.
"So... how much did you save?"
He smiled.
"Eighty bucks."

I picked up the receipts.
Oil.
Filter.
Drain pan.
Ratchet.
Socket.
Funnel.
Creeper.
Gloves.
Shop towels.
Flashlight.
Wheel chocks.
Brake cleaner.
Extra oil because he'd spilled some.
Replacement driveway cleaner because his wife insisted.

I did the math.
"George... you spent four hundred sixty-three dollars and eighty-two cents."
He thought for a moment.
Then smiled.
"Yeah... but next time it'll only cost me eighty."

I couldn't argue with that.
Mostly because I was still trying to figure out how he'd managed to leave an oil handprint on the mailbox.

I've known George a long time.
He's taught me that saving money and spending money are sometimes very closely related.
Especially when confidence arrives about thirty minutes before experience.

After all...
...I've already got the socket he lost.
