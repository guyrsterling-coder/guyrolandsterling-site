Reflections
So You Would Become A Preacher?
Go And Sell All Your Possessions, (vanities) and ‘Come and Follow Me’
“Never man spake like this man.” —John 7:46
By Guy Roland Sterling
The Scope of This Study

A red-letter index of the King James Version identifies 638 verses containing words of Jesus in Matthew, 289 in Mark, 573 in Luke, 423 in John, and 17 in Acts. Those numbers include parallel accounts of the same teachings. They also depend upon editorial choices about where the words of Jesus begin and end.

Acts contains additional words of the risen Lord that some red-letter systems do not mark. These include His conversation with Ananias, His warning to Paul to leave Jerusalem, His command to go to the Gentiles, His encouragement in prison, and a saying remembered by Paul that does not appear in the four Gospels: “It is more blessed to give than to receive” (Acts 9:10–16; 20:35; 22:18–21; 23:11).

The purpose here is not to reproduce every saying. It is to examine the whole record and identify the manner, methods, aims, and principles that appear again and again.

The Great Pattern

Jesus did not teach merely to pass along information.

His teaching followed a greater pattern:

  1. Reveal God.
  2. Announce His kingdom.
  3. Reveal Himself as the way to the Father.
  4. Expose the condition of the human heart.
  5. Call people to repentance, faith, and obedience.
  6. Form disciples who would carry the message to others.

In one sentence:

Jesus received the message from the Father, spoke in the power of the Spirit, opened the Scriptures, revealed the kingdom, exposed the heart, called for a decision, and trained obedient witnesses.

That is the pattern we must follow.

A Necessary Warning

We may learn from the preaching of Jesus. We cannot copy everything about it.

Jesus possessed authority within Himself.

We do not.

Jesus knew every human heart perfectly.

We do not.

Jesus could say, “But I say unto you,” because He is Lord over the Law.

We must say, “The Scripture says.”

Jesus preached Himself because He is the Savior, the King, the Bread of Life, the Good Shepherd, the Resurrection, and the only way to the Father.

We must never preach ourselves. We preach Him.

His authority was inherent. Ours is borrowed. His knowledge was perfect. Ours is limited. His words were without error. Ours must constantly be tested against Scripture. Matthew says that the people were astonished because Jesus taught as one having authority, not as the scribes. Yet Jesus also declared that His teaching came from the Father and that He sought the Father’s glory rather than His own.

We must therefore reflect His manner without pretending to possess His divine privileges.

What Jesus Taught

Although Jesus addressed many subjects, His teaching centered upon several great truths.

1. The Father

Jesus revealed the Father’s character, love, holiness, mercy, authority, care, and will.

He taught people to pray, “Our Father.” He spoke of the Father who sees in secret, feeds the birds, clothes the lilies, forgives, judges, seeks worshippers, gives the Spirit, and receives returning children.

2. The Kingdom of God

The kingdom stood at the center of His public preaching:

“The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel.”

Jesus described the kingdom through seeds, soil, treasure, pearls, nets, vineyards, servants, weddings, lamps, banquets, children, kings, debts, and harvests.

The kingdom was present in the King, yet its final fullness remained future.

3. His Own Identity

Jesus did not present Himself as one teacher among many.

He taught that He is:

  • The Son of Man
  • The Son of God
  • The Messiah
  • The Lord of the Sabbath
  • The Bread of Life
  • The Light of the World
  • The Door
  • The Good Shepherd
  • The Resurrection and the Life
  • The Way, the Truth, and the Life
  • The True Vine
  • The coming Judge and King

Everything turned upon what people did with Him.

4. Repentance and Faith

Jesus did not preach self-improvement.

He called people to repent, believe the gospel, receive the kingdom, become as little children, be born again, and follow Him.

5. The Human Heart

Jesus moved beyond outward conduct.

Murder begins in anger. Adultery begins in lust. Defilement comes from within. Words reveal the heart. Treasure directs the heart. Fruit reveals the tree.

His teaching attacked sin at its root.

6. Love

The whole Law rests upon love for God and love for one’s neighbor.

That love includes enemies, strangers, sinners, the weak, the poor, the rejected, and those who cannot repay us.

7. Discipleship

Following Jesus requires surrender.

A disciple must deny himself, take up his cross, count the cost, remain in Christ, continue in His word, and place loyalty to Him above every competing claim.

8. Mercy, Justice, and Faithfulness

Jesus condemned religion that counted small details while neglecting mercy, justice, faith, humility, and love.

9. Prayer and Dependence upon God

He taught persistence, faith, forgiveness, humility, secrecy, submission, watchfulness, and confidence in the Father.

10. Money and Possessions

Jesus spoke often about wealth because money reveals allegiance.

One cannot serve God and mammon. Possessions may become a master. Riches can deceive. Giving can expose faith. Greed can destroy the soul.

11. Judgment and Eternal Life

Jesus spoke plainly about resurrection, accountability, separation, heaven, hell, reward, punishment, and His return.

Love did not make Him silent about judgment.

12. Mission

His disciples were called to become fishers of men, proclaim the kingdom, make disciples, teach His commandments, and witness to Him among all nations.

The Rules of the Master Teacher Rule 1: Receive the message before trying to deliver it

Jesus did not speak from Himself.

He said:

“My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me.”

He also said the Father had commanded Him what to say and what to speak.

The first duty of a teacher is not creativity. It is fidelity.

Do not begin with:

“What would make a good sermon?”

Begin with:

“What has God said?”

Principle: We are messengers, not inventors.

Key passages: John 7:16–18; 8:28; 12:49–50; 14:10, 24.

Rule 2: Seek God’s glory, not your own

Jesus identified a dangerous teacher:

“He that speaketh of himself seeketh his own glory.”

A sermon may mention God while still displaying the preacher.

The desire to sound clever, profound, scholarly, powerful, entertaining, or original can quietly replace the desire to glorify God.

The true teacher wants the hearer to leave thinking about Christ—not the speaker.

Principle: The moment the messenger becomes the attraction, the message has been weakened.

Rule 3: Depend upon the Holy Spirit

Jesus entered His public ministry “in the power of the Spirit.” At Nazareth He declared:

“The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel.”

After His resurrection, He commanded the disciples to wait for the promised Spirit before becoming His witnesses.

Knowledge, structure, delivery, and personality are not enough.

Principle: Do not attempt spiritual work through human strength alone.

Key passages: Luke 4:1, 14, 18; Acts 1:4–8.

Rule 4: Pray before major decisions and demanding ministry

Jesus withdrew to pray.

He prayed before selecting the Twelve. He prayed before Peter’s confession. He prayed before the cross. He prayed alone after periods of intense ministry.

His prayer life was not a public performance. It was the hidden foundation of His public strength.

Principle: The unseen prayer must support the seen ministry.

Key passages: Mark 1:35; Luke 5:16; 6:12–13; 9:18; 22:41–44.

Rule 5: Know the Scriptures well enough to use them naturally

During temptation, Jesus answered with Scripture.

At Nazareth, He read Scripture and declared its fulfillment.

When challenged, He asked:

  • “Have ye not read?”
  • “What is written in the law?”
  • “How readest thou?”
  • “Did ye never read in the Scriptures?”
  • “Have ye not read that which was spoken unto you by God?”

After His resurrection, He opened Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms and showed how they testified of Him.

Scripture was not decoration attached to His message. It formed the message.

Principle: The Bible must govern the sermon, not merely appear inside it.

Key passages: Matthew 4:4–10; 12:3–7; 19:4–6; 21:16, 42; 22:29–32; Luke 4:16–21; 24:25–27, 44–47.

Rule 6: Explain the meaning, not merely the words

Jesus did not quote Scripture and stop.

He uncovered its purpose.

He showed that the Sabbath was made for man. Mercy stood above empty ritual. Marriage reached back to God’s creation. The commandments reached into the heart. David’s words revealed the greatness of the Messiah.

Principle: Reading the text is not the same as teaching the text.

A faithful teacher must explain:

  • What it says
  • What it means
  • Why it matters
  • How it reveals God
  • How it points to Christ
  • What obedience requires
Rule 7: Teach the whole purpose of God, not isolated rules

Religious leaders often reduced God’s Word to regulations.

Jesus restored the larger purpose.

He joined commandments to love. Sabbath law to mercy. Giving to motive. Prayer to relationship. Marriage to creation. Purity to the heart.

Principle: Never teach a command apart from the character and purpose of the God who gave it.

Rule 8: Keep the kingdom and the King at the center

Jesus did not build His message around personal success, national pride, emotional comfort, financial increase, or self-esteem.

He proclaimed God’s reign.

The kingdom called people away from self-rule and under the authority of God.

Principle: Biblical preaching must confront the question: Who rules this life?

Rule 9: Preach Christ rather than yourself

Jesus could place Himself at the center because He is the center.

We place Him there because we are not.

The preacher is not the hero of the sermon. Personal stories may serve the truth, but the truth must never become a stage for the speaker.

Principle: Use yourself as a witness, never as the Savior.

Rule 10: Make the central truth unmistakable

Jesus could teach with great depth, but His central demands were plain:

  • Repent.
  • Believe.
  • Follow Me.
  • Fear not.
  • Watch.
  • Pray.
  • Forgive.
  • Love.
  • Go.
  • Do likewise.
  • Sin no more.
  • Be ready.

He did not bury the main point beneath endless explanation.

Principle: A hearer should be able to state the message after the teaching ends.

Rule 11: Speak with conviction

Jesus did not present divine truth as a possibility among many equal opinions.

He said:

  • “Verily, verily, I say unto you.”
  • “But I say unto you.”
  • “Ye must be born again.”
  • “Except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish.”
  • “No man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

His certainty did not come from temperament. It came from truth.

Principle: Speak humbly about yourself. Speak confidently where Scripture is clear.

Rule 12: Do not confuse authority with loudness

Jesus possessed unmatched authority, yet described Himself as meek and lowly in heart.

Authority is not shouting, intimidation, aggression, or theatrical force.

True authority rests upon truth, character, and faithfulness.

Principle: Volume cannot create authority. Neither can a title.

Rule 13: Speak to the heart, not merely the conduct

Jesus refused to settle for outward improvement.

He addressed anger beneath murder, lust beneath adultery, greed beneath possessions, pride beneath prayer, unbelief beneath questions, and hypocrisy beneath religion.

Principle: Do not merely trim bad fruit. Expose the root.

Ask:

  • What does this sin reveal?
  • What is the person loving?
  • What is he fearing?
  • What is she trusting?
  • What has taken God’s rightful place?
Rule 14: Use ordinary life to explain eternal truth

Jesus drew lessons from:

  • Seed and soil
  • Birds and flowers
  • Bread and water
  • Sheep and shepherds
  • Fishing nets
  • Lamps and houses
  • Children and families
  • Wages and debts
  • Weddings and funerals
  • Vineyards and harvests
  • Lost coins
  • Lost animals
  • Lost sons
  • Weather
  • Building
  • Business
  • Farming
  • Courts
  • Travel

People remembered His teaching because they encountered its images again in daily life.

Principle: Build a bridge from what people know to what they need to understand.

Jesus used many forms—poetry, proverbs, parallel statements, exaggeration, parables, similes, metaphors, riddles, irony, paradoxes, and questions—to make truth striking and memorable.

Rule 15: Tell stories that carry truth

Jesus did not tell stories merely to entertain.

His parables exposed hearts, overturned assumptions, revealed the kingdom, confronted hypocrisy, and demanded judgment.

The Good Samaritan forced a lawyer to reconsider the meaning of “neighbor.”

The Prodigal Son exposed both open rebellion and hidden self-righteousness.

The Rich Fool exposed greed.

The Pharisee and the Publican exposed pride.

The Sower exposed the hearer.

Principle: A story must serve the truth. Never let the story become the entire point.

Rule 16: Let the hearer see himself inside the message

Many of Jesus’ parables quietly drew listeners into making a judgment. Only then did they realize that they had judged themselves.

That happened with the wicked husbandmen. It happened with Simon the Pharisee. It happened when Jesus questioned the lawyer after telling of the Samaritan.

Principle: The strongest application is often the truth the hearer recognizes before the teacher states it.

Rule 17: Ask questions that expose the heart

Jesus asked questions such as:

  • “What seek ye?”
  • “Why are ye fearful?”
  • “Whom say ye that I am?”
  • “What think ye of Christ?”
  • “Wilt thou be made whole?”
  • “Lovest thou me?”
  • “Why callest thou me, Lord, Lord, and doest not the things which I say?”
  • “What shall it profit a man?”
  • “Which now of these three was neighbour unto him?”
  • “Where are those thine accusers?”
  • “Why persecutest thou me?”

His questions searched motives, exposed contradictions, corrected assumptions, invited confession, and forced decisions.

Principle: Do not answer every question too quickly. Sometimes the hearer first needs a better question.

Rule 18: Answer the person beneath the question

Some questions were sincere.

Others were traps.

Jesus responded differently.

Nicodemus asked about signs. Jesus addressed his need for new birth.

The Samaritan woman spoke about water. Jesus addressed her thirst, her life, and her need for true worship.

The lawyer asked, “Who is my neighbour?” Jesus exposed his attempt to limit his duty.

The Pharisees asked about divorce. Jesus returned to God’s purpose in creation.

Principle: Listen for the need, fear, pride, wound, or motive hidden beneath the words.

Rule 19: Adapt the method without changing the truth

Jesus did not speak to everyone in the same manner.

He spoke differently to:

  • Crowds
  • Disciples
  • Children
  • Sinners
  • Religious rulers
  • Grieving families
  • Political authorities
  • Hostile questioners
  • Curious seekers
  • Proud experts
  • Broken individuals

The truth remained unchanged. Its delivery fitted the person and moment.

Principle: Faithfulness does not require sameness.

Rule 20: Teach people according to what they can presently bear

Mark says Jesus spoke the word to the crowds “as they were able to hear it.” Later, He explained matters privately to His disciples.

Near the end of His ministry, He told them:

“I have yet many things to say unto you, but ye cannot bear them now.”

Truth may need sequence.

Milk comes before strong meat. Foundations come before upper stories.

Principle: Do not weaken truth. Order it wisely.

Key passages: Mark 4:33–34; John 16:12.

Rule 21: Combine public proclamation with private explanation

Jesus taught multitudes. Then He explained difficult matters to His disciples in private.

The pulpit cannot accomplish every part of discipleship.

Some truths require:

  • Conversation
  • Questions
  • Correction
  • Repetition
  • Personal application
  • Time

Principle: Public preaching gathers people. Personal teaching forms them.

Rule 22: Use memorable structure

Jesus often used:

  • Two roads
  • Two trees
  • Two houses
  • Two masters
  • Sheep and goats
  • Wise and foolish
  • First and last
  • Losing and finding
  • Giving and receiving
  • Exaltation and humility
  • Death and life

He also used repeated patterns:

  • Ask, seek, knock
  • Blessed are…
  • Woe unto you…
  • You have heard…but I say…
  • He that hath ears to hear, let him hear

These structures carried truth into memory.

Principle: Truth deserves form. Good structure helps the mind retain what the heart has heard.

Rule 23: Use contrast to sharpen the choice

Jesus rarely left people with a fog of possibilities.

There was:

  • A narrow way and a broad way
  • Good fruit and bad fruit
  • Rock and sand
  • Light and darkness
  • God and mammon
  • Faithfulness and wickedness
  • Wisdom and foolishness
  • Life and destruction

Principle: Show the difference between obedience and disobedience. Do not blur what Jesus made clear.

Rule 24: Use strong language for serious danger

Jesus spoke of plucking out an eye, cutting off a hand, swallowing a camel, moving a mountain, passing a camel through a needle’s eye, and straining at a gnat.

These were not empty attempts to shock people. The force of the image matched the seriousness of the truth.

Principle: Strong language must serve a strong truth—not the teacher’s desire for attention.

Rule 25: Teach through visible actions

Jesus sometimes turned an action into a sermon.

He:

  • Placed a child in the midst
  • Washed His disciples’ feet
  • Received the rejected
  • Touched the unclean
  • Ate with sinners
  • Cleansed the temple
  • Took bread and a cup
  • Asked for a coin
  • Watched a widow give
  • Wrote upon the ground
  • Allowed a sinful woman to wash His feet

His conduct interpreted His words.

Principle: The teacher’s life is always teaching—even when his mouth is closed.

Rule 26: Live what you preach

Jesus could say:

“I have given you an example.”

No gulf existed between His message and His life.

He taught service and washed feet.

He taught forgiveness and forgave His killers.

He taught prayer and prayed.

He taught sacrifice and gave Himself.

He taught love and loved to the end.

Principle: The life of the teacher either confirms the sermon or contradicts it.

Rule 27: Show compassion without weakening truth

Jesus was moved with compassion because people were like sheep without a shepherd.

He touched lepers. Received children. Fed the hungry. Wept with mourners. Defended the shamed. Welcomed sinners.

Yet compassion never caused Him to deny sin.

He could say both:

“Neither do I condemn thee”

and:

“Go, and sin no more.”

Principle: Grace does not pretend sin is harmless. Truth does not treat wounded people as enemies.

Rule 28: Treat broken sinners differently from hardened hypocrites

Jesus could be remarkably gentle with those crushed by sin.

He could be terrifyingly direct with those who used religion to hide pride, greed, cruelty, and unbelief.

To the burdened: “Come unto me.”

To hypocrites: “Woe unto you.”

To the ashamed: mercy.

To predators: warning.

To the humble: hope.

To the proud: exposure.

Principle: Do not crush the bruised reed. Do not flatter the wolf.

Rule 29: Confront hypocrisy plainly

Jesus condemned:

  • Public righteousness hiding private corruption
  • Long prayers used as a mask
  • Giving for applause
  • Religious titles used for status
  • Tradition used to cancel God’s command
  • Leaders who burdened others
  • Teachers who loved honor
  • Men who appeared clean while remaining corrupt within

Principle: Religious appearance must never receive protection at the expense of truth.

Rule 30: Never use the pulpit to seek applause

Jesus warned against praying, giving, and fasting “to be seen of men.”

He refused to perform signs for entertainment. He withdrew when crowds sought to make Him king for the wrong reason. When hard truth caused followers to leave, He did not change the message to win them back.

Principle: Popularity is not proof of faithfulness. Rejection is not proof of faithfulness either. The test is truth.

Rule 31: Do not soften difficult truth to keep the crowd

John 6 records many disciples turning back after a hard teaching.

Jesus did not run after them with a safer version.

He turned to the Twelve and asked whether they would also leave.

Principle: Never make the truth needlessly offensive. Never remove its offense to protect attendance.

Rule 32: Do not answer every attack

Jesus sometimes answered hostile questions.

Sometimes He answered with another question.

Sometimes He told a story.

Sometimes He exposed the trap.

Sometimes He remained silent.

He did not allow an enemy to control the conversation merely by asking a question.

Principle: Silence may be wiser than entering a dishonest argument.

Rule 33: Correct error directly

Jesus told the Sadducees:

“Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God.”

He corrected false beliefs about:

  • Resurrection
  • Marriage
  • Sabbath
  • Purity
  • Wealth
  • Leadership
  • Prayer
  • Tradition
  • Messiah
  • The temple
  • The kingdom

Principle: Love does not leave serious error untouched.

Rule 34: Be patient with slow learners

The disciples misunderstood parables, miracles, His death, His resurrection, servant leadership, faith, and the nature of the kingdom.

Jesus corrected them. Repeated Himself. Explained again. Asked questions. Gave examples. Rebuked when needed. Then continued teaching them.

Principle: Repetition is not failure. Some truths must be heard many times before they take root.

Rule 35: Call for a decision

Jesus did not leave truth hanging in the air.

His teaching ended with calls such as:

  • Follow Me.
  • Go and do likewise.
  • Repent.
  • Believe.
  • Watch.
  • Pray.
  • Forgive.
  • Sell what stands between you and God.
  • Take up your cross.
  • Come unto Me.
  • Keep My commandments.
  • Feed My sheep.
  • Go into all the world.

His Sermon on the Mount ended with two builders. Hearing without doing meant building upon sand.

Principle: Teaching is incomplete until the hearer knows what obedience requires.

Rule 36: Aim for obedience, not admiration

People were astonished at Jesus’ teaching.

Astonishment alone was never the goal.

The wise man was not the person who admired the saying. He heard and did it.

Principle: A sermon has not succeeded merely because people liked it, praised it, shared it, or remembered it. The question is whether they obeyed God.

Rule 37: Give clear next steps

When Jesus called people, His instructions were often concrete:

  • Follow Me.
  • Stretch forth thy hand.
  • Arise, take up thy bed, and walk.
  • Go, wash.
  • Show thyself to the priest.
  • Sell, give, come, follow.
  • Go home and tell.
  • Feed My sheep.
  • Wait in Jerusalem.
  • Go into the city.
  • Speak and hold not thy peace.

Principle: Do not end with vague spiritual feeling. Show what faithfulness looks like next.

Rule 38: Train disciples through practice

Jesus did not keep the Twelve as permanent spectators.

He called them to be with Him. Then He sent them to preach.

They received instructions, authority, responsibility, opposition, experience, and correction.

The seventy were also sent out. They returned and reported what had happened. Jesus then corrected their excitement by directing their joy toward salvation rather than power.

Principle: Hear, observe, practice, report, correct, and send again.

Rule 39: Prepare people for rejection and suffering

Jesus warned His messengers that some would refuse them.

They would face hatred, persecution, betrayal, false accusations, imprisonment, and death.

He did not sell discipleship through false promises of ease.

Principle: A teacher who promises obedience without cost has not taught the way Jesus taught.

Rule 40: Teach servant leadership

The world’s rulers exercise authority over others.

Jesus said it must not be so among His disciples.

The greatest must become servant. The first must become last. The leader must care for the weak. The Master washed feet.

Principle: Christian authority bends down. It does not climb over people.

What the Risen Jesus Adds in Acts

The manner of Jesus did not change after His ascension.

His recorded words in Acts sharpen several principles for the church.

1. Wait for the promised power

Before witnessing, the disciples had to wait for the Holy Spirit.

Activity was not a substitute for power from God.

Acts 1:4–5

2. Be witnesses, not performers

Jesus said:

“Ye shall be witnesses unto me.”

A witness tells what he knows to be true. He does not become the center of the testimony.

Acts 1:8

3. Begin near, then move outward

The mission moved from Jerusalem to Judaea, Samaria, and the uttermost part of the earth.

Principle: Do not use concern for distant people to avoid faithfulness to those nearby.

4. Ask questions that expose sin

To Saul, Jesus asked:

“Saul, Saul, why persecutest thou me?”

The question forced Saul to face what he was doing and whom he was attacking.

Jesus identified Himself before giving Saul his next instruction.

5. Connect Christ with His people

By persecuting believers, Saul was persecuting Jesus.

Principle: Christ identifies Himself with His church. How people treat His people matters to Him.

6. Call people personally and direct them specifically

Jesus called:

“Ananias.”

He called:

“Saul, Saul.”

He gave each man a definite assignment.

Ananias had to go to Saul. Saul had to rise, enter the city, receive instruction, bear Christ’s name, and suffer for it.

Principle: Calling must lead to obedience.

7. Speak despite fear

At Corinth, the Lord told Paul:

“Be not afraid, but speak, and hold not thy peace: for I am with thee.”

Courage rested not upon Paul’s personality, but upon Christ’s presence.

Acts 18:9–10

8. Define the purpose of gospel preaching

Jesus told Paul that his ministry would:

  • Open eyes
  • Turn people from darkness to light
  • Turn them from Satan’s power to God
  • Bring forgiveness of sins
  • Bring an inheritance among the sanctified
  • Call people to faith in Christ

That is one of Scripture’s clearest descriptions of the purpose of gospel ministry.

Acts 26:16–18

9. Send the message beyond comfortable boundaries

Paul wanted to remain among his own people.

Jesus said:

“Depart: for I will send thee far hence unto the Gentiles.”

The messenger does not choose his mission merely by comfort, familiarity, or acceptance.

Acts 22:18–21

10. Strengthen the messenger in the conflict

In Jerusalem, Jesus stood beside Paul and said:

“Be of good cheer.”

Paul’s work was not finished. As he had testified in Jerusalem, he would also testify in Rome.

Principle: The presence of opposition does not mean the mission has failed.

11. Support the weak

Paul preserved a saying of Jesus not recorded in the four Gospels:

“It is more blessed to give than to receive.”

This saying was connected with labor, generosity, and supporting the weak.

Acts 20:35

The Balance Found in Jesus

Jesus held together truths that we often separate.

He was:

  • Authoritative, yet meek
  • Simple, yet profound
  • Compassionate, yet uncompromising
  • Patient, yet urgent
  • Personal, yet universal
  • Doctrinal, yet practical
  • Scriptural, yet never dry
  • Memorable, yet never theatrical
  • Gracious to sinners, yet severe toward hypocrisy
  • Clear enough for children, yet deep enough to confound scholars
  • Focused upon eternal life, yet attentive to hunger, grief, sickness, money, family, work, and daily need

His manner cannot be reduced to one style.

He was not always quiet.

Not always forceful.

Not always indirect.

Not always plain.

Not always gentle.

Not always severe.

Perfect love determined the manner required by the person, truth, danger, and moment.

The Master Teacher’s Test

Before teaching or preaching, ask:

  1. Is this message drawn from Scripture? Or am I using Scripture to support something I already wanted to say?
  2. Does it reveal God? Will people understand more of His character, will, holiness, mercy, and purpose?
  3. Does it point to Christ? Or does the speaker, church, movement, controversy, or moral lesson occupy the center?
  4. Have I explained the text faithfully? Have I considered its context, audience, purpose, and place within the whole counsel of God?
  5. Have I reached the heart? Or have I dealt only with outward behavior?
  6. Is the central truth clear? Could a hearer repeat it in one sentence?
  7. Is the form helping the truth? Do the stories, examples, questions, and illustrations serve the Word—or distract from it?
  8. Am I seeking God’s glory? Would I still preach this if nobody praised me?
  9. Have I spoken with both truth and compassion? Have I protected the wounded without protecting sin?
  10. Have I identified error plainly? Or have I hidden behind vague language to avoid opposition?
  11. Have I shown the hearer what obedience requires? What must be believed, confessed, stopped, started, surrendered, forgiven, restored, or endured?
  12. Does my life agree with the message? Where must the teaching first correct the teacher?
  13. Have I depended upon the Holy Spirit? Or have I trusted preparation, personality, education, emotion, or technique?
  14. Am I training people to follow Christ without me? True teaching does not create dependence upon the teacher. It forms disciples of Jesus.
The Final Rule

Jesus did not merely deliver sermons.

He gave Himself.

His words carried authority because truth, character, love, obedience, sacrifice, and mission were perfectly joined in Him.

Therefore, the final principle is this:

Become what you teach.

Let the Word first search the teacher.

Let repentance begin in the pulpit.

Let prayer precede proclamation.

Let private life support public speech.

Let compassion govern tone.

Let Scripture govern content.

Let Christ remain the center.

Let obedience remain the goal.

Let the Spirit supply the power.

Then speak—not to display knowledge, build a following, win applause, or protect a reputation.

Speak so that eyes may open.

Speak so that darkness may give way to light.

Speak so that captives may hear of freedom.

Speak so that sinners may receive forgiveness.

Speak so that believers may become disciples.

Speak so that disciples may become witnesses.

Speak so that God may be glorified.

For never did a man speak like this Man.
